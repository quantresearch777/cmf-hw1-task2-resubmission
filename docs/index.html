03 · research process

What we investigated

The research was deliberately broader than the final code. The goal was to find transferable improvements, not simply the highest number after many trials.

Open-course benchmark

Public course repositories were compared by numerical results, validation design, reproducibility, and implementation choices.

Rule families

Symbol gates, liquidation pressure, maker/taker direction, q90/q95/q99, 30/60s windows, size bands, and combinations.

Classical ML

Thirty-one CPU configurations spanning linear, tree, boosting-style, calibration, clustering, and feature-increment branches.

1 · Broad screenGenerate plausible causal candidates.
2 · Past to futureCalibration selects; the following month evaluates.
3 · Exact recomputationFinalists run on the full stream without sampling.
4 · Audit and freezeBootstrap, multiplicity, capacity, tails, and API tests.
Research outcome. The useful Task2 increment was structural and simple: separate ETH from BTC at 300s. Runtime ML added only a small unstable increment. For Task3, extra combinations failed to transfer, so the strongest responsible change was a train-only selection reconstruction.
04 · anti-overfit validation

Why this is more than choosing the best backtest

A large research search can create false winners. The final candidates therefore had to pass a sequence of controls before they were frozen.

1 · Causal inputsOnly information available at decision time is used, including the specified event delay.
2 · Select on pastTask2 uses calibration month → next month; Task3 uses Dec-Jan → February.
3 · Exact streamReported finalists are recomputed on every eligible event, not a convenient sample.
4 · UncertaintyFive-day block bootstrap preserves time dependence; sign tests and tail metrics are reported.
5 · Search auditAll 96 Task2 comparisons enter BH correction; failed ML and rule branches remain disclosed.

Task2 transfer check

Each calibration month independently selects the symbol gate. The gate is then frozen and scored on the next month. All four next-month deltas are positive.

Task3 selection check

One deterministic rule selects q/window settings using December-January only. February is used for evaluation, not for changing the challenger.

Honest boundary. These controls materially reduce post-hoc fitting risk, but previously inspected months cannot become a pristine holdout. That is why the presentation reports corrected statistics and asks for one final hidden-period test with no further parameter changes.

07 · decisions and limits

What the resubmission proves - and what it does not

Not promoted

  • runtime ML after the ETH gate;
  • Task3 per-symbol q/window combinations;
  • Task3 symbol gating and size bands;
  • late 30–60s reaction windows;
  • synthetic sampling as a replacement for exact results.

Supported claims

  • Task2 exact 300s improves by +22.23 bps;
  • Task2 deltas are positive in 6/6 months and 4/4 next-month folds;
  • both Task3 files are causal at event availability;
  • the Task3 challenger follows a deterministic train-only selector.
Evaluation boundary. March-April for Task2 and February for Task3 had already been inspected. This is strict retrospective walk-forward evidence, not a newly untouched holdout. Fresh hidden data remains the decisive next test.
